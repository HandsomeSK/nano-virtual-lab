## Unified 3D Lab

The second 18 October 2026 brief supersedes the earlier four-model Lab. All interactive scientific WebGL models belong to `/lab`, in three continuous modules: Graphene atomic structure (`#graphene`), MoS₂ atomic structure (`#mos2`), and MoS₂ transistor & interface engineering (`#transistor`). All three modules and their essential controls are directly present. There are no model-category tabs, hidden whole-model accordions, extra model directory or navigation-only substitutes for a viewer.

Two fixed-material AtomicWorkbench instances and one DeviceViewer own separate local options and cameras. The shared device uses one ScientificStage with a constant SceneSpec; structural state changes toggle prebuilt geometry and materials within its existing scene. Channel OFF/ON and structure Standard / Without carbon dots / With carbon dots are independent. Standard has an ideal continuous dielectric. The no-carbon discontinuous oxide is an explicitly study-specific conceptual comparison; ON/OFF illustrates charge without calculating current. Switching structure preserves valid selection, layer visibility, camera, ON/OFF, explosion and label options. Removing the interface also removes stale interface-hidden state and falls back to dielectric selection; camera focus safely ignores removed geometry. Device Reset always restores Standard, OFF, all layers and the initial camera, irrespective of the entry link.

The four legacy device fragments resolve to the same instrument: `#transistor` and `#interactive-device` choose Standard; `#interface-engineering` and `#interactive-stack` choose With carbon dots. The component listens to initial location, hashchange and popstate, deduplicates unchanged location notifications, and releases listeners on unmount. A repeated explicit link to the same current URL still reapplies its intended structural entry state. It does not write URL/history or initiate its own scrolling; the shared navigation manager owns those operations. Browser back/forward therefore keeps native restoration, and state-only hash notifications do not trigger a second scroll.

Legacy material query URLs render the complete Lab without hiding or reordering models. On initial entry or an explicit cross-route commit, `/lab?material=graphene` or `/lab?material=mos2` without a fragment gains its matching existing section and focuses it. `history.replaceState` preserves query parameters and history state without adding an entry. Explicit fragments take precedence; unknown material values and back/forward restoration remain untouched. `#atomic-structure` remains valid, and existing fragment IDs on Device, Why MoS₂ and the interface article still identify their static explanations.

Every model exposes selected material, key dimensions, essential controls and a short immediate explanation. A single default-closed ModelExplanation contains its complete cited physics and limits. Native details/summary supplies keyboard expansion. Its named region is keyboard-focusable and scrollable to a 300 px maximum on fine-pointer desktop screens, without clipping the underlying content. At 900 px and below or with a coarse pointer, it grows within normal document flow. The device has one unified “Materials, scale & model limits” section covering operation and interface engineering; it does not duplicate the earlier two sets of model notes.

Viewers mount within 200px of the viewport and remain mounted until leaving a wider 900px boundary, avoiding repeated initialization from small scrolls. Far-away viewers release their renderer while their module controls and camera state remain mounted. Static previews preserve the document layout. Rendering is on demand; offscreen or hidden-document transitions pause. Cleanup releases renderer resources, animation frames, animations, observers and listeners. This is viewport-based residency, not a promised fixed maximum of WebGL contexts. Failure to load WebGL keeps a labeled static fallback and scientific explanations available. See [the architecture record](docs/unified-3d-lab.md) for implementation ownership and verification scope.

Device, Why MoS₂ and the interface article retain static scientific diagrams, complete theoretical explanations, citations and existing non-3D activities. Device retains its experimental micrograph and component roles. The interface article retains the attributed EOT plot, measurements, primary source and limits. Original conceptual diagrams remain explicitly distinct from experimental images and data.

- The homepage's decorative silver particles drift continuously in normal motion mode. The owner's latest instruction removes the pause control. System reduced motion freezes the scene and hidden/offscreen rendering pauses without resetting its phase.
- Foundations retains all six science topics, sliders, selectors, Fermi/transport activities, disclosures and the FET state toggle. Material controls keep their native button/keyboard behavior.

- Lab retains orbit/zoom/reset/focus, selection, visibility, assembly/explosion and local scientific controls in three independently stateful modules. Keyboard arrows and camera buttons provide alternatives to dragging; touch orbit is opt-in so page scrolling remains available. Full yaw and above/below pitch work in the shared scientific viewers. Material examples, geometry citations and model limits stay beside the corresponding model.
- Simulator input changes recalculate immediately. Existing normalization and five parameter meanings do not change. Save records a bounded versioned scenario in browser-local storage; errors remain visible. Reset restores the existing defaults.
- Comparison retains A/B selection, shared scales, overlay/side-by-side layouts, parameter/readout table, edit links and saved-scenario removal.
